🎯 Why this matters

"Am I normal?" is the question every founder quietly asks and never gets answered. You feel your costs are heavy or your clients pay slowly — but heavy compared to what? The skill answers in one prompt:

1

Your real ratios

Fixed-cost share, software subscriptions, observed client payment delay, revenue seasonality, cash in days of expenses — computed from the Qonto account.

2

The official reference

French public statistics (INSEE structural data, payment delays) fetched live through the Datagouv MCP, at the finest NAF level available.

3

Statistical honesty

Every comparison carries source + vintage + granularity. No exact dataset → one level up, stated. A figure is never made up.

4

You vs you

A temporal self-benchmark ("you, a year ago") computed every time — also the full fallback without Datagouv or outside France.

Typical output: "your clients pay you at 38 days; the sector median is 51 — you collect faster than your peers" / "your fixed costs weigh 61 % vs ~45 %: that's where to dig." Would someone use this on a Monday morning? It's the quarterly Monday — the one where you ask how the business is really doing.

⚙️ How it works

Holds up on messy data

  • The core of the skill is a golden rule: source + vintage + granularity on every comparison ("NAF 62.02A, INSEE Ésane, 2023 data") — or no figure at all
  • NAF class not published → comparison at division level (62 instead of 62.02A), announced on the very line it affects; no reference at all → internal value only, marked "no public reference found"
  • NAF missing from account data → asked or inferred from the activity, and confirmed before use — the sector is never guessed silently
  • Cash-flow categories: list_cash_flow_categories returns 403 on the claude.ai connector — classification via recurrence detection + labels instead
  • Client payment delay computed on Qonto-issued invoices only — disclosed when the user invoices elsewhere; SaaS subscriptions: detected list shown, correctable in one sentence
  • History < 12 months → seasonality tagged "unavailable" rather than computed on noise; card settlement delays handled (emitted_at vs settled_at); pagination ≤ 50 everywhere

🔒

Zero writes, by design: the skill only reads — the Qonto account on one side, public open data on the other. Nothing to approve, nothing that can move. The only "risk" here is statistical, and it's handled by the golden rule: source + vintage + granularity on every figure, or no figure at all.

📐 The 5 ratios of the check-up

RatioComputation (Qonto data)Sector reference
Fixed-cost shareRecurring debits (normalized counterparty, amount stable ±10 %, ≥ 3 hits) ÷ total debits, 12 monthsCost structure by NAF (INSEE Ésane)
Software subscription weightRecurrences whose counterparty matches SaaS patterns ÷ total debits — detected list shown, correctableOrder of magnitude by NAF division when published; otherwise internal value only

Revenue seasonalityCoefficient of variation of monthly receipts over 24 months + peak/trough monthsSector activity indices when available; otherwise self-benchmark
Cash in days of expensesConsolidated balance ÷ average daily debits (12 months)Mostly temporal (you vs you) — little reliable public reference, and the skill says so

🛡 Guardrails

  • 100 % read-only: no write tool is ever called — nothing to approve, nothing that can move money
  • Never invents a sector figure: no dataset → the line says so; degraded granularity → announced; vintage always printed
  • Bank-data ratios ≠ accounting records: an honest approximation, restated in every report — the accountant's figures prevail
  • Sector confirmed, not assumed, when the NAF code isn't in the account data
  • A quarterly/annual check-up, not a daily-piloting tool — for day-to-day: qonto-ceo-cockpit
  • IBANs masked (last 4 digits) · pagination ≤ 50 everywhere
