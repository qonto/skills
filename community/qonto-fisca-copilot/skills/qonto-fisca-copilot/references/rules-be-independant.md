## 🔔 Transaction-Triggered Nudges

### Reverse Charge / Autoliquidation on Foreign B2B Services (UNIVERSAL)

**Universal rule:** Applies even if you benefit from VAT exemption (franchise). Intra-EU B2B services trigger reverse charge on your end.

**Your mechanism:**
- You are the **customer** receiving a service from an EU supplier (non-resident or resident).
- You must **self-assess VAT** in Belgium (not the supplier).
- **Do NOT pay VAT to the supplier.** Reverse charge means you account for VAT via your quarterly VAT return.
- On the supplier's invoice, confirm wording: *"Autoliquidation – Art 51 § 2, 5° Code TVA"* (or similar reference to reverse charge).

**What triggers it:**
- Services (consulting, design, hosting, etc.) supplied by non-established EU provider.
- Domestic construction work, investment gold, CO2 allowances (separate domestic reverse-charge goods).

**Source:** ✅ [Belgium Reverse Charge Manual – Marosa](https://marosavat.com/vat-manual-chapters/belgium-reverse-charge); ✅ [FPS Finance VAT Manual](https://finance.belgium.be/en/enterprises/vat/vat-obligation/vat-exemption-scheme-small-businesses)

### E-Invoicing Mandate (B2B) — January 1, 2026

**Requirement:** ✅ All invoices from one Belgian VAT-registered business to another **must be electronic** (structured format via Peppol).

**What is not allowed:** Paper invoices, unstructured PDFs, email attachments.

**Filing:** Invoices are **not** submitted in real-time to the tax authority—you send/receive them peer-to-peer over Peppol, but you keep records for compliance.

**Grace period:** ✅ 3 months (Jan–Mar 2026, conditional). **Only applies if you can demonstrate good-faith compliance efforts.** Full enforcement from April 1, 2026 onward.

**Penalties:** ✅
- 1st breach: €1,500
- 2nd breach: €3,000
- Further breaches: €5,000 each

**B2C (invoices to consumers):** Not mandated; paper/PDF still acceptable.

**Source:** ✅ [Belgium B2B E-Invoicing Mandate 2026 – Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/e-invoicing-in-belgium-as-from-1-january-2026-key-provisions-of-the-long-awaited-royal-decree/); ✅ [Marosa E-Invoicing Guide](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/e-invoicing-b2b-belgium-complete-guide-january-2026)

### Invoice Required to Deduct or Justify Expenses

**Core rule:** No invoice/receipt = no deduction. Must be:
1. **Dated** during the tax year
2. **Linked to professional activity**
3. **Documented** (invoice, receipt, bank statement, or other proof)

**Professional deductions (real expenses):** Travel, equipment, office rent, software, professional insurance, training, workspace costs.

**Quarterly social contributions:** Payment proof required if claiming early payment or contesting amounts.

**Mileage:** IF claiming travel costs, document trips (start/end, purpose, km). Flat-rate or actual fuel/vehicle depreciation both allowed, but must be **consistent and documented**.

**Source:** ✅ [SPF Finances – Professional Expenses](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/particuliers/declaration-impot/revenus/revenus-professionnels); ✅ [Liantis Deductible Expenses Guide](https://www.liantis.be/fr/je-suis-independant/remuneration/frais-professionnels)

## 🎯 Reliefs / Niches to Activate

### Flat-Rate vs. Real Expense Deduction — Annual Choice

**You can choose each year; it is not locked in.**

#### Flat-Rate (Forfait) Deduction

**For self-employed workers (indépendants-travailleurs):** ✅
- Deduction: **30% of gross professional income**, capped at **€6,070**
- Automatic; no documentation required.
- Best for small turnovers where 30% > actual costs.

**For business owners (indépendants-exploitants):** ✅
- Deduction: **3% of gross professional income**, capped at **€3,200**
- Much lower; most will prefer real expenses.

#### Real (Actual) Expense Deduction

- Deduct **all documented professional expenses** incurred during the tax year.
- No percentage cap; limited only by what you can prove and what is genuinely professional.
- Requires invoices, receipts, bank statements.
- If your expenses exceed the flat-rate ceiling, real expenses are typically superior.

**Example decision:**
- Gross income €30,000; actual expenses €8,000 (documented).
  - Forfait: 30% × €30,000 = €9,000 ✓ (better)
- Gross income €10,000; actual expenses €2,500 (documented).
  - Forfait: 30% × €10,000 = €3,000 ✓ (better)
- Gross income €20,000; actual expenses €7,500 (documented).
  - Forfait: 30% × €20,000 = €6,000 capped; Real: €7,500 ✓ (better)

**Source:** ✅ [SPF Finances Professionnels – Déductions](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/particuliers/declaration-impot/revenus/revenus-professionnels); ✅ [Accountable Expenses Guide](https://www.accountable.eu/fr-be/blog/les-frais-professionnels-forfaitaires/)

### Minimum Social Contribution (Relief for Low-Income Starters)

**First-time starters (primo-starters):** ✅ Reduced minimum social contribution in the first year.
- Q1: €459.82
- Q2–Q4: €890.42 each

**Standard regime (after first year):** ✅ Minimum €890.42 per quarter (excluding social fund management fees of 3–4%).

- These minimums apply **regardless of income**—you pay even if your business made a loss.
- **Upside:** The minimum doesn't scale with turnover; small earners benefit here.

**Source:** ✅ [Xerius/Acerta Social Contribution Schedules 2026](https://media.xerius.be/sites/default/files/documents/2025-12/XER-10630-Barema-Eindklant-en-Zelfstandige-2026_FR_LR%20.pdf); ✅ [Novadesko Blog – Cotisations 2026](https://novadesko.com/fr/blog/fiscalite-independant-belgique-2026-cotisations-ipp); ✅ [Accountable – Social Security Contributions Self-Employed Belgium](https://www.accountable.eu/en-be/blog/social-security-contributions-self-employed/)

### VAT Exemption Threshold

**Annual turnover limit (excl. VAT):** ✅ **€25,000**

- If you stay **below** €25,000, you can opt into the **VAT exemption regime** (franchise TVA).
- No VAT charged to customers; no VAT return filed.
- **Upside:** Simpler compliance.
- **Downside:** Cannot reclaim input VAT on purchases; reverse charge still applies on your intra-EU service purchases.

**If you exceed €25,000:**
- You must **de-register** from the exemption and enter the **standard VAT regime**.
- You then charge/file VAT normally.

**Re-entry:** Must wait a full calendar year before reapplying; re-entry possible from January 1st of the second year after de-registration.

⚠️ **Pending change (not yet law):** Belgium approved raising this to €30,000 in April 2026, but as of July 2026 it remains pending final parliamentary approval and publication. Current law is €25,000.

**Source:** ✅ [FPS Finance – VAT Exemption for Small Businesses](https://finance.belgium.be/en/enterprises/vat/vat-obligation/vat-exemption-scheme-small-businesses); ⚠️ [VATupdate – Belgium Threshold Change](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/04/06/belgium-raises-vat-exemption-threshold-for-small-businesses-to-eur-30000/) (pending legislative action)
